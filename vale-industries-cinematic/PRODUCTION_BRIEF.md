## 1. Character List

### ADRIAN VALE
- **Age:** 47
- **Physical description:** Tall (6'2"), lean, athletic build. Silver-streaked dark brown hair, combed back, no visible grey stubble — clean-shaven. Sharp jawline, pale skin, cold pale-blue eyes. Moves with deliberate, unhurried confidence.
- **Wardrobe:** Immaculately tailored black suit (peak lapel, single-breasted), crisp white dress shirt, no tie — top button undone. Black leather Oxford shoes, polished. Slim silver wristwatch. No jewelry otherwise.
- **Role:** CEO / owner of Vale Industries. The power at the top of the building. Outwardly composed and pleasant; the scene's tension comes from the ambiguity of whether his curiosity about the guard is genuine or performative. Deuteragonist / scene antagonist by implication.
- **Voice/performance note:** Calm, measured, faint smile — never raises his voice. Questions land softly but carry weight.

### THE GUARD
- **Age:** 58
- **Physical description:** Average height, slightly stooped posture from years on his feet. Greying hair cropped short, tired lined face, kind eyes. Slight weariness in his stance — a man who has stood in the same spot for a long time.
- **Wardrobe:** Old navy-blue security uniform, slightly faded and ill-fitting (sleeves a touch long), scuffed and worn black shoes, plain security cap or none (director's choice — recommend no cap for facial visibility), name badge on chest (no legible text needed), analog wristwatch.
- **Role:** Night-shift security guard at the HQ entrance. The emotional center of the story. His quiet honesty in the final line is the point of the piece.
- **Voice/performance note:** Soft-spoken, slightly guarded at first ("Mr. Vale"), warms briefly, then delivers the final line ("I used to") with quiet, unforced honesty — no bitterness, just truth.

## 5. Generation Order

Generate in this sequence — each stage locks the references the next stage depends on:

1. **Characters** — Build a character reference sheet for each of Adrian and the Guard first (front, 3/4, profile; neutral expression + one emotive variant). Lock these before anything else so every later shot stays visually consistent.
2. **Locations** — Generate the three environments (L1 exterior, L2 lobby, L3 elevator) as clean establishing plates, empty of characters, from the specific angles needed in the shot list above.
3. **Still scenes** — Composite/generate each of the 14 clips as a key still frame (character reference + location reference + framing/action from the breakdown table). Review the full sequence as a storyboard before moving on — this is the cheapest point to catch consistency errors.
4. **Color grade** — Apply the grade (Section 7) uniformly to all 14 still frames. Confirm the look holds across day/night contrast, skin tones, and the rain/glass reflections before animating.
5. **Video** — Animate each graded still into its clip (image-to-video), using the timing column above as target duration per clip. Assemble in sequence, add ambient rain/room tone and the dialogue audio last.

## 6. Aesthetic Direction

The film should feel like a modern corporate noir: cold, glassy, and vertical, dominated by the wet-black glow of a night-time skyscraper — deep shadows and sharp architectural lines outside give way to a hushed, minimal lobby inside where every reflection (marble floor, glass walls, elevator doors) doubles the light source and makes the space feel simultaneously grand and airless; camera movement is slow and deliberate (push-ins, static holds) to mirror the guard's stillness against Adrian's unhurried authority, with close-ups reserved for the emotional beats (the phone screen, the hesitation, the final line) so the visual scale contracts as the conversation gets more personal.

---

## 7. Color Grading

**Grade:** Cool corporate-noir teal/blue-black grade with restrained warm accents.

- **Shadows/blacks:** Crushed toward blue-teal, not pure neutral — keep detail in the rain and glass but let deep shadow areas read cold.
- **Midtones:** Desaturated, slightly cool; skin tones pulled just warm enough to stay natural against the cool environment (avoid full teal-orange split — this is subtler than a blockbuster grade).
- **Highlights:** Practical lights (lobby lighting, elevator interior, phone screen) allowed to run warm/amber as the only warm accent in the frame — this is the one visual "human" note against the cold architecture.
- **Contrast:** High but not crushed — retain shadow detail in rain and reflections; avoid pure black clipping except in the final cut-to-black.
- **Saturation:** Low overall (~-15 to -20%) except for isolated warm highlights.
- **Texture:** Subtle film grain, soft highlight bloom on glass/rain reflections, slight vignette on close-ups to isolate faces.
- **Reference feel:** Think *Blade Runner 2049* lobby scenes crossed with a quieter, more restrained *Succession*-style corporate interior — moody, expensive, a little lonely.

Apply this grade as a single consistent LUT/preset across all 14 still frames before animation, so the video pass never has to reconcile mismatched color between clips.
